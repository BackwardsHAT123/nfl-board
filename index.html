True probabilities use a stand-in line, not a book line: each player's season average per game rounded to x.5 (shown as "o" for over). The chance of going over is a normal curve centered on the matchup-adjusted mean (QB: projected yards; receiver: his average receptions scaled by the QB's projected yards over his average) with the player's own game-to-game spread; the parlay multiplies the two legs as if independent, which understates it a little because a QB's yards and his top target's catches move together. With 7 to 9 games the spreads are rough. Projections are simple averages, not lines; compare them with the book's numbers yourself. Quarterbacks known to be out are removed from the table (players_out.csv), since their replacements do not have enough games to rank. When the #1 target is known to be out, the row uses the QB's next-highest-share wide receiver or tight end who is playing, labeled "next up": his target share is scaled up by half of what the out player's targets would add if fully redistributed, and his average and 90% floor are scaled the same way. That is an estimate, not an observed rate. Informational, not a bet signal.

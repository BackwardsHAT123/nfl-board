QB and receiver parlays, Week 9

Each row is a 2-leg combo: the QB's projected passing yards (his completions per game times the opponent's yards per completion allowed) plus his #1 target's projected receptions (target share times the QB's completions per game). Only quarterbacks facing one of the 10 defenses that allow the most yards per completion are listed, sorted by projected yards. The 90% columns show the most yards (QB) or receptions (receiver) he has reached in at least 90% of his games this season (a QB's games with 10+ attempts; a receiver's games he appeared in alongside that QB). True probabilities use a stand-in line, not a book line: each player's season average per game rounded to x.5 (shown as "o" for over). The chance of going over is a normal curve centered on the matchup-adjusted mean (QB: projected yards; receiver: his average receptions scaled by the QB's projected yards over his average) with the player's own game-to-game spread; the parlay multiplies the two legs as if independent, which understates it a little because a QB's yards and his top target's catches move together. With 7 to 9 games the spreads are rough. Projections are simple averages, not lines; compare them with the book's numbers yourself. Informational, not a bet signal.
